Phase 1: The digital ignition

The Screen-to-Classroom Trajectory

While K-Dramas have been dominating Indian OTT viewership in recent years, the Hallyu Wave’s immersion has transcended into the academic sphere. What was once a casual curiosity has now become the catalyst of why Indian students are considering higher education and career opportunities outside the Anglosphere.

According to the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) Outbound Student Migration Corridor Dataset, the number of Indian students travelling to South Korea rose sharply from 526 in 2020 to 1,095 in 2024, more than doubling in four years.

Netflix India’s Weekly Top 10 data further validates this trajectory: Korean titles regularly accounted for 20% to 40% of listed productions, maintaining massive engagement.

Phase 3: Market presence

Commercial Footprints

The reach of Korean culture is equally visible in India’s hyper-local food delivery apps and beauty shelves, creating a structural consumer framework around the academic wave.

Datum Intelligence reports that South Korean imports represent a massive 34% of Blinkit's instant-noodle sales, with Samyang’s iconic Buldak ramyeon series single-handedly driving 18% of that weight.

Simultaneously, a joint Datum Intelligence–Kindlife report projects India’s K-beauty market to balloon from USD 400 million in 2024 to USD 1.5 billion by 2030, scaling active buyers from 11.9 million to over 27 million.

Beyond the Screen

Interviews with Korean-language teachers conducted through the Republic of Korea’s Embassy in India suggest that parents are actively encouraging young people to pursue opportunities in Korea as traditional Anglocentric education routes become increasingly saturated.
